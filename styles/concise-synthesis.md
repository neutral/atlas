# Concise synthesis

## Accounts

One central Tree owns the project's integrated argument: what follows from
taking the other accounts together, where they pull against each other, and
what remains unresolved. Each supporting Tree owns a complete argument
within its scope, including its own conclusions. The central Base argues
from those accounts; a directory or a summary of them is not a synthesis.

A claim is explained in full in the account that owns it. The central
account states what several claims establish together, says no more than
they support, and links down to them. A Facet says how another account
strengthens, limits or challenges a claim: what that account holds, and what
follows here. A new supporting Tree is warranted when a contribution to the
integrated argument needs a sustained account of its own.

## Depth

The Atlas carries each claim, its essential reason and the qualifications
that limit it, and names the place in a source that holds the extended
argument. Reading sources is an expected part of understanding a subject in
depth. Exact requirements, procedures, code and raw evidence stay in the
sources, which keep authority over them. A Point grows when readers
repeatedly have to reconstruct the same connection from the sources.
