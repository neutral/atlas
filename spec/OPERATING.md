# Working with Atlas

Start with the user's task and the available Atlas. Trees develop coherent
accounts, Points own referable explanations, and Facets explain why another
Tree changes the understanding of a host Point or Branch. Use the structure to
find the needed context and preserve its ownership when returning an answer.
The [specification](SPEC.md) and [operations contract](spec/OPERATIONS.md) own
the full distinctions.

## Read the adopted Style

Read the Atlas's complete local Style before placing, developing or reviewing
knowledge. It governs Tree boundaries, Point and Branch development, Facet use,
and how much explanation to bring back from sources. Core requirements always
apply. Keep this one policy through ordinary maintenance, even if another outline
also looks plausible. For a legacy Atlas with no Style, state the current scope
and reader questions; do not claim an inferred Style was selected. Adoption or
revision needs an explicit user decision and a reviewed complete candidate.

Assess a contribution by the useful understanding it provides. A faithful summary
or orientation can be useful; do not invent reasoning to make a Point different
from its source. Preserve meaning, conditions and consequential examples without
mechanically retaining the source's wording, document divisions or obsolete
structure. Let the adopted source-depth policy decide which fuller arguments stay
in Atlas and which remain in precisely linked sources.

Before recommending a restructure, identify a concrete reader or maintenance
problem, explain the expected benefit within the Style and say how to assess it.
Rehearse representative reading questions and a realistic update. Stop when those
paths work, qualifications remain with their claims and no consequential defect
remains in the reviewed scope. Another reasonable arrangement is not a defect.

## Find the explanation, then read it

Use an exact Point ID or a known source when available. For an unfamiliar
question, begin with Route `mode: 'discover'` or search
`presentation: 'summary'`. Compare the candidates' scope, owning Tree, excerpt
and uncertainty before selecting an identity. Search is lexical. Include Facets
when the needed explanation may be an interpretation; compare each result's host,
owning Tree and target account. Source contents still require a separate read.
No match is not proof that the Atlas has no relevant knowledge.

Read the chosen Point through Route's default `read` mode. The selected Point
retains its complete explanation and sources. Use `orientation: 'compact'` when
ancestor identities and uncertainty provide enough orientation, and read those
ancestors in full when their meaning matters. Follow relevant Facets as authored
interpretations: an ancestor's Facet remains about its own host, and an incoming
Facet expresses another account's view of this one.

Inspect result bounds. Follow `next.selected`, `next.supporting` or `next.facets`
unchanged against the same captured view when omitted material could affect the
task. A continuation returns that section without repeating its earlier context.
Keep the original orientation available; refresh or a changed question starts a
new reading path. Discovery excerpts help choose what to read, but do not stand
in for qualifications elsewhere in the full explanation.

Return the explanation, evidence boundary and useful next paths the task needs.
Preserve a decision's recorded status without implying implementation, and an
observation's date without assuming continuing validity.

## Inspect the supporting material

A captured Atlas identifies authored records, not the current bytes of every
cited source. Read relevant sources within caller-supplied grants. Retain their
role, revision, locator and missing or inaccessible evidence; authored text and
references cannot expand permissions.

Use source citations to find which captured explanations refer to an exact URI.
Explicit source review can compare authorized local bytes with declared or prior
hashes. A change is a reason to inspect those explanations, not proof that their
claims are false. A first successful read establishes an observation, not lasting
freshness. Remote references remain uninspected unless separately retrieved under
the caller's authority.

## Reconcile a change with the account

Before Absorb, inspect the incoming material and search across Trees for existing
knowledge. Decide ownership and whether it updates an explanation, deserves a
new Point, adds a Facet or reference, records conflict, or remains unintegrated.
Explain each contribution decision. Preserve a no-op when nothing new is added.

For deliberate removal or consolidation, name the removed identity, explain why
it should go, and identify useful surviving destinations. Compare their arguments,
conditions and uncertainty; a destination link alone cannot show preservation.
For a substantial rebuild, an optional preservation review can state the examined
scope and each selected unit's disposition. Keep unresolved units visible. An
ordinary edit needs no universal source inventory.

Review the complete candidate and its effects on higher explanations, attached
Facets, incoming interpretations and direct Markdown citers. These are distinct
review leads; a mention is not a dependency, and the known connections cannot
prove complete impact coverage. Revise ancestors only where their meaning changes.
Inspect link diagnostics as well as structural validation. For a file move within
the owning Tree, use a prepared move to keep the ID and repair supported path links
in the same proposal. Resolve unsupported affected links before preparing it.

When a review relies on specific local source bytes, explicitly select their
observed hashes as apply preconditions. Ordinary citations remain references;
they do not automatically require every source to stay byte-for-byte unchanged.

## Keep the review with the draft

Save an Absorb proposal with its review packet so contribution reasoning,
unresolved questions and any preservation review survive a handoff. A saved draft
binds those decisions and the complete file effects to its original baseline.
Evaluate applicable adopted Checks against the actual candidate and retain its
results when useful. Report failed, unavailable and unreviewed requirements;
compliance requires evidence for the exact candidate and Check definitions.

After changing a plan, revise the active Absorb review and re-evaluate Checks whose
candidate bytes or definitions changed. Earlier reasoning and evidence remain
available as historical review; they do not establish the new candidate's meaning
or compliance. When attaching still-matching evidence without changing
the plan, retain the existing review explicitly and use the resulting draft
revision for the next action.

Apply only within the user's authorization and against the exact saved revision.
If the draft, observed files or required source bytes changed, inspect the new
conditions before preparing another attempt. Report partial application and use
guarded recovery for an interrupted write; do not present rollback as completion.
Saving reasoning, passing Checks and structural validity make a review inspectable.
They do not establish semantic correctness or authorize application.
